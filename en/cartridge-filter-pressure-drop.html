What causes pressure drop in an industrial cartridge filter?

Short answer: Record inlet and outlet pressure at comparable flow and temperature, then verify valve positions, gauge condition, cartridge configuration, operating hours, and cleaning history. A rising differential may indicate increasing resistance through the filter path, but the cleaning or replacement limit is not universal: use the approved cartridge datasheet and the actual line conditions.

Problem: “low pressure” can mean three different things

Operators may use “pressure drop” to describe lower outlet pressure, a larger inlet-to-outlet differential, or reduced product flow. These are not the same condition. Record inlet pressure, outlet pressure, and actual line capacity at the same time before drawing a conclusion.

Behriz Mobadel's cartridge-filter configuration includes inlet and outlet pressure gauges so both sides of the housing can be monitored.

Insight: compare against the line's own healthy baseline

Record a stable baseline for the same product: flow, temperature, inlet pressure, outlet pressure, cartridge rating, and operating hours. Compare later readings under similar conditions.

Do not copy a generic pressure limit. The approved limit depends on the media datasheet, liquid, flow rate, and project design. A universal limit is not confirmed in Behriz Mobadel's public technical records.

Solution: a step-by-step troubleshooting checklist

1. Make the readings comparable

  • Record the product or liquid and production batch.
  • Record flow and temperature with both pressure readings.
  • Verify inlet, outlet, drain, and bypass valve positions against the baseline.

2. Read both gauges simultaneously

Calculate the difference between inlet and outlet readings. A trend collected over several checks is more useful than a single reading.

3. Check instruments and the flow path

  • Check that both gauges return and respond correctly.
  • Review valves, piping, drain status, and visible leakage.
  • If the change started immediately after service, review cartridge installation and sealing.

4. Match the cartridge configuration

Verify rating, length, quantity, and cartridge type against the line design. Confirmed Behriz Mobadel options include 2, 5, and 10 micron ratings; 12, 18, and 24 inch housing lengths; and 5- or 9-cartridge arrangements. Final selection is project-specific.

5. Add operating and cleaning history

Record operating time since service and the latest CIP or backwash. The Behriz Mobadel housing includes a CIP path and backwash capability using water and compressed air. Cleaning sequence and operating limits must follow the approved project instructions.

Frequently asked questions

How is cartridge-filter pressure drop identified?

Read inlet and outlet pressure under comparable operating conditions and record the difference. Use the approved cartridge datasheet for the final allowable limit.

Does higher differential pressure always mean immediate replacement?

Not necessarily. Verify flow, temperature, valves, gauges, and cleaning history before deciding.

Is there one universal allowable value?

No universal value is published here; it depends on media, liquid, flow, and project design.

What should be sent for engineering review?

Send liquid type, line capacity, both pressure readings under the same conditions, temperature, cartridge details, operating hours, and cleaning history.

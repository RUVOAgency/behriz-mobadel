How do you reduce dissolved oxygen in beverage process water?

Reliable deaeration starts with actual inlet and outlet conditions, not with a nameplate flow rate alone.

Required engineering data
Short answerOne industrial method uses controlled vacuum conditions to separate oxygen and other dissolved gases from process water. A cold-water deaerator should be configured around flow, temperature, inlet dissolved oxygen, outlet target, vacuum conditions and the plant cleaning program.

Why dissolved oxygen control matters

Process water can carry oxygen and other dissolved gases into downstream beverage production. Controlled deaeration supports oxidation management, flavor and product-quality stability, and water preparation for processes such as CO₂ injection. The target value must be defined for the actual product and process.

How vacuum deaeration works

  1. Water inlet: Water enters at a defined flow rate and temperature.
  2. Vacuum generation: Lower pressure enables dissolved gases to separate from the water.
  3. Controlled contact: The internal configuration manages contact between water and the low-pressure space.
  4. Deaerated-water outlet: Water with reduced dissolved oxygen moves to the next process stage.
  5. Controls and cleaning: Instrumentation, automation logic and CIP integration are specified for the project.
Engineering boundary: Outlet oxygen is not independent of line conditions. Water temperature, inlet oxygen, vacuum stability and equipment configuration directly affect performance.

Behriz Mobadel cold-water deaerator: general range

The published general range covers 15,000-25,000 L/h, AISI 304L or 316L product-contact materials, fully automatic operation and integration with the plant CIP program. Outlet dissolved oxygen may be reduced to approximately 0.2 ppm or near zero, but the final value is confirmed only after reviewing project conditions and issuing the technical data sheet.

Common deaerator selection mistakes

  • Selecting on nominal flow alone
  • Not defining inlet and target dissolved oxygen
  • Ignoring temperature changes between shifts
  • Under-specifying vacuum stability and instrumentation
  • Leaving CIP routes, drainage and installation limits undefined

Frequently asked questions

Is cold-water deaeration only used for carbonated soft drinks?

No. It can be evaluated for process water in soft-drink, malt-beverage, juice and other beverage lines where dissolved oxygen control is relevant to product quality.

Is 0.2 ppm guaranteed for every line?

No. It is a general published performance range. Final performance depends on inlet conditions and project configuration.

Should the equipment use 304L or 316L?

Material selection must be matched to water and product composition, CIP chemistry and operating conditions. Final selection requires engineering confirmation.

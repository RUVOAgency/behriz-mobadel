Homogenizer mixer vs process mixer: how to choose

Selection should begin with the process duty: does the line only need formulation and mixing, or must it also reduce coarse particles and deliver uniform homogenization?

Published: 31 October 20268-minute readB2B procurement guide
In this guideShort answerSelection problemPublished dataDecision methodRFQ checklistFAQ
Short answer: For formulation, preparation and product mixing, assess a process/formula mixer. When the duty includes reducing coarse particles, producing an emulsion or suspension, or uniform homogenization, assess a homogenizer mixer. Product name alone does not close the decision; sample, flow or batch size, concentration, temperature and acceptance criteria are required.

Problem: why “we need a homogenizer for sauce” is not a complete RFQ

A product may require circulation, dissolution, formula mixing, particle reduction or homogenization at different process stages. If the duty is not defined, suppliers can propose different equipment under the same word “mixer.”

A comparable RFQ should state the current product problem and the acceptable result: what must be dissolved, dispersed, reduced or made uniform, and how the result will be measured.

A published capacity range is not a project sizing guarantee. No public official figure is confirmed for power, speed, particle size, batch time or product-stability claims; this page therefore does not state one.

Solution: close the choice with five questions

  1. What is the process objective? Formula mixing only, or particle reduction and homogenization too?
  2. What enters the mixer? Record ingredients, concentration, temperature, solids and addition sequence.
  3. How does the line operate? State batch volume or continuous flow, operating hours and allowable cycle time.
  4. How will output be accepted? Define a reference sample, sampling method and measurable criterion.
  5. What limits cleaning and installation? Provide cleaning chemistry, downtime, footprint and connections to vessel or pump.
Technical boundary: “Sauce” or “emulsion” alone does not prove that one configuration is correct. Suitability must be confirmed from the actual formulation, concentration, temperature, process objective and acceptance test.

Industrial mixer RFQ checklist

  • Product name, available formula and all products sharing the line
  • Line capacity or batch volume and batches per day
  • Concentration, inlet temperature and process-temperature range
  • Incoming particle condition and outlet target
  • Ingredient addition sequence and duration
  • Cleaning method and allowable downtime
  • Footprint, fixed/mobile requirement and automation level
  • FAT/SAT acceptance criterion and sampling responsibility

If particle size, speed, power or cycle time is not yet established, mark it “test and engineering confirmation required” instead of inserting an assumed value.

Frequently asked questions

Is a homogenizer mixer better than a process mixer?

Neither is universally better. Process duty and outlet acceptance criteria determine which path should be assessed.

What is the Behriz homogenizer-mixer capacity range?

The published range is 50–50,000 L/h. Final project capacity requires confirmation.

What is the process-mixer capacity range?

The published range is 50–35,000 L/h.

Can a homogenizer mixer be used for sauce?

Published duties include food processing, emulsions and suspensions; final suitability depends on formula data and a product test.

What is required for a quotation?

Kieselguhr filter vs candle filter: how to choose for juice and malt beverage

Published applications overlap, but nameplate flow alone cannot close the choice. Start with the actual product, production cycle and measurable outlet acceptance criterion.

Published: 7 November 20269-minute readB2B procurement guide
In this guideShort answerSelection problemPublished dataDecision methodRFQ checklistFAQ
Short answer: Published Behriz data lists both the Kieselguhr plate filter and candle filter for syrup, juice and malt beverage. Neither is universally better. Select from product type, normal and maximum flow, inlet turbidity or solids, outlet target, cycle time, cleaning, footprint and an agreed acceptance method.

Problem: why nameplate flow is not enough

Two quotations may cover the same line flow while using different filtration cycles, cleaning downtime and outlet assumptions. A catalogue range is an initial screen; it does not prove net cycle capacity or product quality for a real feed.

A comparable RFQ must describe the current problem: inlet turbidity or solids, acceptable outlet, required run length and allowable cleaning downtime.

These ranges do not establish equal cycle time or outlet quality. No public project value is confirmed here for filter-aid grade or dose, flux, effective area, allowable differential pressure, net cycle capacity, utility use or outlet turbidity; this page therefore states no assumed figure.

Solution: close the choice in three stages

  1. Define feed and target. Record product, temperature, inlet solids or turbidity and a measurable outlet criterion.
  2. Compare the real cycle. Put normal/maximum flow, production duration, wash time and required net capacity on the same basis.
  3. Specify acceptance. Agree sample, test method, sampling points, FAT/SAT scope and approval responsibility before order.
Technical boundary: “Juice” or “malt beverage” alone is not a final selection criterion. Filter-aid grade and dose, flux, area, differential pressure, final CIP/backwash sequence, utilities and outlet criteria require project engineering confirmation.

RFQ checklist for a comparable quotation

  • Product type and every product sharing the line
  • Normal and maximum flow, or batch volume and daily schedule
  • Temperature, inlet turbidity or solids condition, and available sample
  • Outlet target and agreed measurement method
  • Target run length and allowable wash downtime
  • Footprint and fixed/mobile requirement
  • Available utilities and waste-disposal limits
  • Automation, instruments and data-recording requirement
  • FAT/SAT criteria, sampling points and approval responsibility

Where filter-aid dose, flux, effective area, differential pressure, water use or cleaning time is unknown, mark it “trial and engineering confirmation required” rather than inserting an assumed number.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Kieselguhr filter better than a candle filter?

Neither is universally better. Product, flow, feed, outlet target, cycle and cleaning determine which proposal is more suitable.

What is the published Kieselguhr-filter capacity?

The published range is 15,000–30,000 L/h; final project capacity requires confirmation.

What is the published candle-filter capacity?

The published range is 4,000–40,000 L/h and is not a selection basis on its own.

Are both used for juice and malt beverage?

Yes. Both list these applications, but final suitability depends on actual product data and an acceptance test.

What is required for a quotation?

Send product type, line capacity, inlet condition, outlet target, cycle, cleaning, footprint, utilities and FAT/SAT criteria.
